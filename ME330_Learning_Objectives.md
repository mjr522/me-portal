# ME 330: Mechanics of Deformable Bodies - Course Learning Objectives

This document compiles the learning objectives for all lesson slide decks in ME 330. For slide decks with explicitly stated objectives, the listed objectives have been directly extracted. For slide decks without explicit objective slides, targeted learning objectives have been generated based on the top 3–4 core concepts presented in each deck.

---

## Lesson 1: Course Overview & Stress/Strain Fundamentals
- Objective 1: Understand the course structure, policies, expectations, and how mechanics of deformable bodies applies to mechanical and aeronautical engineering design.
- Objective 2: Define basic concepts of normal stress ($\sigma$) and normal strain ($\epsilon$) in deformable members subjected to axial loads.
- Objective 3: Distinguish between elastic and plastic material behavior on a stress-strain diagram and identify key material properties like Young's Modulus ($E$).
- Objective 4: Apply fundamental equilibrium equations ($\sum F = 0$, $\sum M = 0$) to evaluate simple structural members.

## Lesson 2: Stress, Strain & Axially Loaded Members
- Objective 1: Calculate normal stress, shear stress, and bearing stress in pin-connected and axially loaded structural connections.
- Objective 2: Determine allowable design stresses using appropriate Factors of Safety ($FS$) for engineering design.
- Objective 3: Calculate axial deformations ($\delta = \frac{PL}{AE}$) in statically determinate members under single and multiple axial loads.
- Objective 4: Apply significant figures and sound problem-solving methodology to mechanical stress analysis problems.

## Lesson 2 (Demo): Shearing & Bearing Stresses Classroom Demonstration
- Objective 1: Differentiate between single shear and double shear loading configurations in bolted and pinned joints.
- Objective 2: Calculate average shear stress ($\tau$) acting on critical shear planes of a connecting pin.
- Objective 3: Determine projected contact areas and calculate average bearing stress ($\sigma_b$) on supporting brackets and pedal linkages.

## Lesson 3: Indeterminate Structures I
- Objective 1: Review and apply the calculation of normal strain due to axial loading using geometry and material behavior.
- Objective 2: Interpret stress–strain diagrams to identify linear elastic behavior and extract the modulus of elasticity for use in axial deformation problems.
- Objective 3: Recognize statically indeterminate axial systems and explain why equilibrium equations alone are insufficient.
- Objective 4: Apply compatibility (deformation) conditions derived from geometry to supplement equilibrium equations in solving statically indeterminate problems.
- Objective 5: Solve basic statically indeterminate axial problems using the superposition method (over-constrained axial members, pivoting bar systems, thermal expansion).

## Lesson 4: Indeterminate Structures II & Thermal Stresses
- Objective 1: Calculate unconstrained thermal deformation ($\delta_T = \alpha \Delta T L$) resulting from temperature changes.
- Objective 2: Formulate compatibility equations for constrained structural members subjected to combined axial loads and thermal expansion.
- Objective 3: Determine thermal stresses developed in over-constrained systems using the principle of superposition.
- Objective 4: Analyze practical engineering applications involving thermal expansion, such as interference fits and structural pins.

## Lesson 5: Indeterminate Structures III (Rotational Rigidity)
- Objective 1: Formulate displacement compatibility relationships for statically indeterminate systems incorporating rigid pivoting members.
- Objective 2: Relate linear bar deflections ($\delta = \frac{PL}{AE}$) to angular rotations ($\theta$) of attached rigid links.
- Objective 3: Solve for internal forces and stress distributions in multi-material composite axial assemblies.
- Objective 4: Apply systematic solution procedures combining statics, material behavior, and geometric compatibility.

## Lesson 6: Generalized Hooke's Law & Poisson's Ratio
- Objective 1: Define Poisson's ratio ($\nu$) and calculate transverse strains resulting from axial elongation or compression.
- Objective 2: Apply Generalized Hooke's Law to calculate multiaxial normal strains ($\epsilon_x, \epsilon_y, \epsilon_z$) under triaxial stress states.
- Objective 3: Determine volumetric strain (dilatation) and changes in volume for materials subjected to hydrostatic or multiaxial stress.
- Objective 4: Differentiate between plane stress ($\sigma_z = 0$) and plane strain ($\epsilon_z = 0$) conditions.

## Lesson 7: St. Venant's Principle & Stress Concentration Factors
- Objective 1: Explain St. Venant's Principle regarding stress distribution uniformity away from localized load application points.
- Objective 2: Identify structural discontinuities (fillets, holes, notches) that cause localized stress concentrations.
- Objective 3: Determine stress concentration factors ($K_t$) from stress charts and calculate peak maximum stress ($\sigma_{\max} = K_t \sigma_{\text{nom}}$).
- Objective 4: Apply stress concentration factors in structural design and factor of safety evaluations.

## Lesson 8: Torsion - Shearing Stress and Strain
- Objective 1: Calculate internal torque ($T$) along circular shafts using free-body diagrams and equilibrium equations.
- Objective 2: Calculate polar moment of inertia ($J$) for solid and hollow circular cross-sections.
- Objective 3: Apply the elastic torsion formula ($\tau = \frac{T r}{J}$) to find maximum shear stress and shear stress distribution.
- Objective 4: Calculate the angle of twist ($\phi = \frac{T L}{G J}$) in uniform and segmented circular shafts subjected to torsional loads.

## Lesson 9: Beams in Bending & Flexure Formula
- Objective 1: Identify pure bending conditions and assumptions for symmetric beam cross-sections.
- Objective 2: Determine the centroid and moment of inertia ($I$) of composite beam cross-sections using the parallel axis theorem.
- Objective 3: Apply the elastic flexure formula ($\sigma = -\frac{M y}{I}$) to calculate maximum tensile and compressive bending stresses.
- Objective 4: Calculate section modulus ($S = \frac{I}{c}$) to compare beam bending efficiency and allowable moment capacity.

## Lesson 11: Eccentric Axial Loading and Bending
- Objective 1: Describe centroids and first moments of area for symmetric and composite beam cross-sections.
- Objective 2: Resolve eccentric axial forces into an equivalent centric axial load and a bending moment.
- Objective 3: Apply the principle of superposition to combine normal stresses from centric axial loading ($\frac{P}{A}$) and flexural bending ($\frac{M y}{I}$).
- Objective 4: Determine the shifted position of the neutral axis where net normal stress is zero under eccentric loading.

## Lesson 12: Unsymmetric Bending
- Objective 1: Understand unsymmetric bending behavior when bending moments do not act along principal axes of symmetry.
- Objective 2: Resolve arbitrary bending moment vectors ($M$) into components along the principal section axes ($M_y, M_z$).
- Objective 3: Calculate combined normal stress at any point on the cross-section using superposition ($\sigma = \frac{M_y z}{I_y} - \frac{M_z y}{I_z}$).
- Objective 4: Determine the orientation angle ($\alpha$) of the neutral axis relative to the principal axes.

## Lesson 13: Beams in Bending & Shear/Moment (V-M) Diagrams
- Objective 1: Classify beam supports (pin, roller, fixed) and determine determinacy and reaction forces.
- Objective 2: Establish standard sign conventions for internal shear forces ($V$) and bending moments ($M$).
- Objective 3: Construct shear force ($V$) and bending moment ($M$) diagrams using the method of sections.
- Objective 4: Identify locations and magnitudes of maximum absolute shear force ($|V|_{\max}$) and bending moment ($|M|_{\max}$).

## Lesson 14: Shear and Bending Moment (V-M) Relations
- Objective 1: Apply differential calculus relations between distributed loads, shear force, and bending moment ($\frac{dV}{dx} = -w(x)$, $\frac{dM}{dx} = V(x)$).
- Objective 2: Construct continuous mathematical functions $V(x)$ and $M(x)$ across beam segments.
- Objective 3: Utilize graphical construction methods to quickly draw $V$ and $M$ diagrams by calculating area under load curves.
- Objective 4: Locate points of zero shear to find local peak bending moments along a beam.

## Lesson 15: V-M Diagram Practice
- Objective 1: Construct $V$ and $M$ diagrams for complex beam configurations with concentrated loads, distributed loads, and applied couples.
- Objective 2: Develop explicit piecewise functions $V(x)$ and $M(x)$ for multi-segment loaded beams.
- Objective 3: Identify critical beam cross-sections requiring detailed bending and shear stress analysis.
- Objective 4: Apply graphical shortcut checks to verify internal consistency of shear and moment diagrams.

## Lesson 16: Transverse Shear Stresses in Beams
- Objective 1: Explain the physical mechanism of horizontal sliding and transverse shear stress ($\tau$) in beams undergoing flexure.
- Objective 2: Calculate the first moment of area ($Q = A' \bar{y}'$) for rectangular, circular, and flanged beam sections.
- Objective 3: Apply the transverse shear formula ($\tau = \frac{V Q}{I t}$) to compute shear stress distribution across beam cross-sections.
- Objective 4: Determine shear flow ($q = \frac{V Q}{I}$) and solve for fastener spacing (nails, bolts, welds) in built-up beams.

## Lesson 17: Stresses on Oblique Planes
- Objective 1: Calculate normal ($\sigma_\theta$) and shear ($\tau_\theta$) stresses acting on inclined planes of axially loaded members.
- Objective 2: Analyze stress transformation relationships as a function of plane orientation angle ($\theta$).
- Objective 3: Determine orientations of maximum normal stress ($\theta = 0^\circ$) and maximum shear stress ($\theta = 45^\circ$).
- Objective 4: Explain why material failure modes (e.g., ductile cup-and-cone vs brittle cleavage) depend on stresses on oblique planes.

## Lesson 18: Plane Stress Transformation I
- Objective 1: Represent a two-dimensional state of plane stress on a stress element ($\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \tau_{xy}$).
- Objective 2: Derive stress transformation equations using free-body equilibrium of a triangular wedge element.
- Objective 3: Calculate transformed normal stresses ($\sigma_{x'}$) and shear stresses ($\tau_{x'y'}$) for any element rotation angle ($\theta$).
- Objective 4: Utilize double-angle trigonometric identities to simplify plane stress transformations.

## Lesson 19: Plane Stress Transformation II & Mohr's Circle
- Objective 1: Determine principal angles ($\theta_p$) and calculate principal stresses ($\sigma_1, \sigma_2$) where shear stress is zero.
- Objective 2: Calculate maximum in-plane shear stress ($\tau_{\text{in-plane}}^{\max}$) and its corresponding orientation ($\theta_s$).
- Objective 3: Construct Mohr's Circle for plane stress using center ($C = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2}$) and radius ($R$).
- Objective 4: Extract principal stresses and transformed stress states graphically using Mohr's Circle.

## Lesson 20: Mohr's Circle Practice
- Objective 1: Construct Mohr's Circle for arbitrary 2D stress states and identify key reference points.
- Objective 2: Determine normal and shear stresses acting on specifically rotated elements using Mohr's Circle geometry.
- Objective 3: Calculate shear and normal stresses along inclined joint surfaces (such as glued scarf splices).
- Objective 4: Transform stress elements between Cartesian coordinates and principal stress orientations.

## Lesson 21: 3D Mohr's Circle & Absolute Maximum Shear Stress
- Objective 1: Construct 3D Mohr's Circles incorporating all three principal stresses ($\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3$).
- Objective 2: Determine the absolute maximum shear stress ($\tau_{\text{abs}}^{\max} = \frac{\sigma_{\max} - \sigma_{\min}}{2}$) for 2D plane stress and 3D stress states.
- Objective 3: Differentiate between out-of-plane maximum shear stress and in-plane shear stress when both principal stresses have the same sign.
- Objective 4: Introduce matrix eigenvalue methods for extracting 3D principal stresses from general stress tensors.

## Lesson 23: Strain Rosettes & Strain Transformation
- Objective 1: Define plane strain components ($\epsilon_x, \epsilon_y, \gamma_{xy}$) and relate them to normal and shear deformations.
- Objective 2: Apply strain transformation equations to compute normal strains in arbitrary directions.
- Objective 3: Utilize strain rosettes ($45^\circ$ rectangular or $60^\circ$ delta) to experimentally measure surface strains.
- Objective 4: Solve systems of strain equations to determine principal strains ($\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$) and maximum shear strain ($\gamma_{\max}$).

## Lesson 24: Thin-Walled Pressure Vessels
- Objective 1: Identify thin-wall criteria ($\frac{r}{t} \ge 10$) for cylindrical and spherical pressure vessels.
- Objective 2: Calculate hoop (circumferential) stress ($\sigma_1 = \frac{p r}{t}$) and longitudinal stress ($\sigma_2 = \frac{p r}{2 t}$) in cylindrical vessels.
- Objective 3: Calculate membrane stress ($\sigma = \frac{p r}{2 t}$) in spherical pressure vessels.
- Objective 4: Construct Mohr's Circles for inner and outer vessel surfaces to evaluate maximum shear stress and strains in vessel walls and welded seams.

## Lesson 25: Combined Loading I
- Objective 1: Identify internal force resultants (axial load $P$, shear force $V$, torque $T$, bending moment $M$) acting on a cross-section under general 3D loading.
- Objective 2: Calculate individual stress components ($\frac{P}{A}, \frac{M y}{I}, \frac{T r}{J}, \frac{V Q}{I t}$) at critical surface points.
- Objective 3: Superimpose normal stresses ($\sigma_x$) and shear stresses ($\tau_{xy}$) to establish total stress states at target points.
- Objective 4: Determine principal stresses and maximum shear stress at critical locations using 2D stress transformation.

## Lesson 26: Combined Loading II (Vector Methods)
- Objective 1: Apply 3D vector cross products ($\vec{M} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$) to determine moment components ($M_x, M_y, M_z$) about cross-sectional centroidal axes.
- Objective 2: Map vector moments to specific stress types (torsional shear vs bending normal stresses).
- Objective 3: Formulate combined stress tensors at multiple evaluation points on solid and hollow shafts.
- Objective 4: Identify governing critical points on a structure by evaluating combined principal stresses.

## Lesson 27: Combined Loading III (3D Worked Examples)
- Objective 1: Solve comprehensive 3D combined loading problems on complex brackets, structural elbows, and cantilever beams.
- Objective 2: Perform free-body cuts at critical cross-sections to establish complete 3D force and moment equivalence.
- Objective 3: Calculate detailed normal stress and shear stress distributions across complex geometries.
- Objective 4: Evaluate overall structural integrity by determining principal stresses and yield safety factors at critical points.

## Lesson 29: Introduction to Finite Element Analysis (FEA)
- Objective 1: Understand fundamental concepts of Finite Element Analysis (FEA) as an approximate numerical method for complex structural elasticity.
- Objective 2: Define basic FEA elements (1D rod/beam, 2D planar/shell, 3D solid elements) and mesh nodes.
- Objective 3: Formulate stiffness matrices and boundary conditions for 1D bar element networks.
- Objective 4: Conduct mesh refinement studies to evaluate numerical convergence and stress concentration accuracy.

## Lesson 30: Beam Deflections by Integration
- Objective 1: Derive the differential equation of the elastic curve ($E I \frac{d^2 v}{dx^2} = M(x)$).
- Objective 2: Formulate appropriate kinematic and force boundary conditions at fixed, pinned, and roller supports.
- Objective 3: Perform successive integrations of $M(x)$ to obtain continuous slope equation $\theta(x)$ and deflection equation $v(x)$.
- Objective 4: Determine constants of integration using boundary conditions and continuity conditions across load intervals.

## Lesson 31: Beam Deflections by Superposition
- Objective 1: Understand the conditions (linear elasticity, small deflections) required to apply the principle of superposition to beam deflections.
- Objective 2: Utilize standard beam deflection tables (Appendix E) for canonical load cases (cantilever and simply supported beams).
- Objective 3: Decompose complex beam loading configurations into combinations of standard reference load cases.
- Objective 4: Calculate total slope ($\theta$) and deflection ($v$) at specified points by summing individual load contributions.

## Lesson 32: Beam Deflection Practice
- Objective 1: Compare integration methods and superposition methods for determining beam deflections.
- Objective 2: Solve multi-load beam deflection problems combining point loads, distributed loads, and concentrated moments.
- Objective 3: Determine maximum deflection locations and magnitudes for simply supported and overhang beams.
- Objective 4: Apply deflection limits to verify serviceability criteria in structural beam design.

## Lesson 33: Statically Indeterminate Beam Deflections
- Objective 1: Identify statically indeterminate beams and determine their degree of redundancy.
- Objective 2: Apply the double integration method to statically indeterminate beams by including redundant reaction forces in the moment equation.
- Objective 3: Apply the method of superposition to statically indeterminate beams by selecting redundant reactions and enforcing zero-displacement compatibility conditions.
- Objective 4: Solve for redundant reaction forces, shear-moment diagrams, and elastic curve equations for propped cantilevers and continuous beams.

## Lesson 34: Statically Indeterminate Beam Deflections Practice
- Objective 1: Master the selection of redundant reaction forces in statically indeterminate beam systems.
- Objective 2: Formulate compatibility equations matching beam deflections to elastic support settlements or rigid constraints.
- Objective 3: Solve complex statically indeterminate beam problems using superposition shortcut methods.
- Objective 4: Calculate reaction forces, internal moments, and maximum deflections in over-constrained beam structures.

## Lesson 35: Exam / GR 3 Review
- Objective 1: Synthesize core mechanics topics including thin-walled pressure vessels, combined loadings, and stress transformations.
- Objective 2: Review beam deflection methods (integration and superposition) for statically determinate and indeterminate beams.
- Objective 3: Calculate principal stresses and maximum shear stresses across multi-axial load scenarios.
- Objective 4: Prepare problem-solving strategies for comprehensive exam assessments.

## Lesson 37: Euler Column Buckling I
- Objective 1: Explain column instability and distinguish between material yielding failure and geometric buckling failure.
- Objective 2: Derive Euler's critical load formula ($P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{L^2}$) for pin-ended columns.
- Objective 3: Calculate critical buckling stress ($\sigma_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E}{(L/r)^2}$) as a function of column slenderness ratio ($L/r$).
- Objective 4: Determine governing buckling direction by evaluating minimum moment of inertia ($I_{\min}$).

## Lesson 38: Euler Column Buckling II (End Conditions & Effective Length)
- Objective 1: Define effective length ($L_e = K L$) and effective length factors ($K$) for various column end conditions (fixed, pinned, free).
- Objective 2: Calculate critical buckling loads ($P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{(K L)^2}$) for columns with fixed-fixed, fixed-pinned, and fixed-free ends.
- Objective 3: Determine whether a compression member fails by Euler column buckling or material yield stress ($\sigma_y$).
- Objective 4: Analyze experimental buckling data and evaluate structural design safety factors for compression columns.

## Lesson 40: Course Summary & Final Exam Review
- Objective 1: Review essential concepts across axial loading, indeterminate structures, torsion, and bending stress analysis.
- Objective 2: Review stress/strain transformations, Mohr's circle, strain rosettes, and thin-walled pressure vessel design.
- Objective 3: Review beam deflection methods, statically indeterminate structures, combined loading vector methods, and column buckling.
- Objective 4: Synthesize overall course knowledge to solve comprehensive, multi-topic mechanics of materials problems on the final exam.
